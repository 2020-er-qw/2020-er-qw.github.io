If you've landed here, you've probably got the same question I had when I started looking for AI tool guides: **why does every article sound like it was written by the tool itself?**

Every post says the same thing — "revolutionary," "10x your productivity," "you won't believe this." Then you try the tool, and it breaks in a way nobody mentioned. Or worse, it quietly wastes two hours of your life and you can't get those two hours back.

This site is my attempt to write the version I wanted to read.

## What this site is

AI Field Notes is a collection of **hands-on notes from someone who uses AI tools for actual work** — writing, image and video generation, and the messy business of building a content workflow that survives contact with real life.

Concretely, that means:

- **Workflows, not listicles.** I'll show you the steps I actually take, including the boring ones and the ones that failed.
- **What breaks, not just what works.** When a tool gets restricted, produces something unusable, or quietly changes its behavior, you'll read about it here.
- **Opinions, with reasons.** I'll tell you when I think something is overhyped, and why. You can disagree — that's fine — but you'll know where I stand.

I write mostly about Gemini for writing, AI image and video tools, and the automation pipelines I use to run multiple content accounts solo. If a tool isn't part of my real workflow, I probably won't write about it.

## What this site isn't

To be equally clear:

- **Not a news feed.** I won't repost every AI announcement. If it matters, I'll write something substantive when I've actually used it.
- **Not a "best tools" ranking.** Those lists age in three months and mostly reward whoever pays for placement.
- **Not a promise of guaranteed results.** Your tools, accounts, and circumstances differ from mine. I share what happened here, not what will definitely happen for you.
- **Not sponsored content.** This site is independent. No sponsorships, no affiliate deals. If I recommend something, it's because I use it.

## The one rule every article follows

Everything here comes from daily use, not from a press release. If I haven't tried it myself, I won't present it as experience — and if I got something wrong, I'd rather correct it than quietly leave it up.

I write these notes for one kind of reader: someone who wants to **actually use** AI tools for real work, not just read about them. If that's you, you're in the right place.

Start with the latest post below, or browse the topics in the sidebar. And if something here saves you an hour, that's a good day's work for both of us.
